LESSON 01

Understanding Personal Finance

Personal finance is the process of managing the money you earn, spend, save and invest.

It is not simply about earning more. Good personal finance is also about making thoughtful decisions about the money you already have.

The basic financial cycle:

Earn → Spend → Save → Invest → Protect

Key areas of personal finance

  • Managing income and everyday expenses
  • Creating a realistic budget
  • Building savings
  • Maintaining an emergency fund
  • Investing for future goals
  • Managing financial risks and debt

The objective is to create a system where your money supports both your current needs and your future goals.

Key takeaway: Financial decisions become easier when you know where your money comes from, where it goes and what you are trying to achieve.
LESSON 02

Income & Expenses

Income is the money you receive. Expenses are the amounts you spend to meet your needs, wants and financial commitments.

Understanding income

Income can come from sources such as salary, business income, freelance work, interest or other legitimate sources.

Understanding expenses

Expenses can include food, transportation, education, bills, entertainment, subscriptions and other purchases.

SIMPLE EXAMPLE

Suppose you receive ₹25,000 in a month and spend ₹18,000. The remaining ₹7,000 can potentially be allocated towards savings, investments or other financial goals.

Simple rule:
If you don't know where your money is going, it becomes difficult to decide where your money should go.
Key takeaway: Knowing your income and expenses gives you a clearer picture of your financial position.
LESSON 03

Budgeting

A budget is a plan for how you intend to use your income over a particular period.

Budgeting helps you understand your spending patterns and allocate money towards important financial goals.

A simple budgeting process

  • Identify your monthly income
  • List your regular expenses
  • Identify discretionary spending
  • Set a savings target
  • Review your actual spending regularly
SIMPLE EXAMPLE

If your monthly income is ₹30,000, you might first account for essential expenses, then allocate a portion towards savings and investments, while keeping some amount for discretionary spending.

A good budget should be realistic. A plan that is impossible to follow is unlikely to remain useful for long.

Key takeaway: A budget is a planning tool, not a restriction. Its purpose is to help you make intentional spending decisions.
LESSON 04

Saving & Emergency Fund

Saving means setting aside part of your income for future needs, goals or unexpected situations.

Why an emergency fund matters

An emergency fund is money kept aside for unexpected financial needs, such as urgent expenses or a temporary interruption in income.

Think before investing:

Your financial plan should account for unexpected expenses before taking on investments that may fluctuate in value.

Savings vs Emergency Fund

General savings may be used for planned goals such as education, travel or a future purchase. An emergency fund is specifically intended for unexpected financial needs.

Key takeaway: Building a financial cushion can help you handle unexpected expenses without immediately depending on debt.
LESSON 05

Introduction to Investing

Investing means putting money into assets with the expectation of generating returns over time.

Examples of investment avenues include stocks, bonds, mutual funds and other financial or real assets.

Saving vs Investing

Saving generally focuses on preserving money for near-term needs, while investing is usually associated with longer-term goals and involves some level of risk.

Remember:
Higher potential returns generally come with greater uncertainty or risk. An investment decision should consider your goal, time horizon and ability to handle losses.

Common investment considerations

  • Investment objective
  • Time horizon
  • Risk tolerance
  • Liquidity needs
  • Expected return
Key takeaway: Investing is not simply about chasing returns. The investment should make sense for the investor's goal and risk profile.
LESSON 06

Risk & Diversification

Risk is the possibility that an investment may produce a result different from what you expected, including losing some or all of the invested amount.

What is diversification?

Diversification means spreading investments across different assets or investments rather than concentrating everything in one place.

SIMPLE EXAMPLE

Imagine an investor puts all of their money into a single investment. If that investment performs poorly, the entire portfolio can be affected.

Holding a range of investments can reduce concentration in one particular investment, although diversification does not eliminate investment risk.

Before making an investment decision

  • Understand what you are investing in
  • Consider your financial goal
  • Consider your time horizon
  • Understand the associated risks
  • Avoid making decisions only because of past returns
Final takeaway:
A strong financial plan connects income, spending, saving, investing and risk management instead of treating each area separately.
Key takeaway: Understand risk before seeking returns, and avoid putting all your financial resources into a single investment.
