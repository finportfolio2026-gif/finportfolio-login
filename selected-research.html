Understanding Risk and Return in Investment Decisions

A practical research note on the relationship between investment risk, expected return and investor decision-making.

FinPortfolio Research · Investment Analysis · Premium

Executive Overview

Investment decisions are rarely based on return alone. Investors also need to understand the uncertainty associated with achieving that return.

The relationship between risk and return is therefore an important foundation of investment analysis. Higher potential returns are generally associated with greater uncertainty, although taking more risk does not guarantee a higher return.

Key Points

  • Return measures the gain or loss generated by an investment.
  • Risk represents uncertainty surrounding investment outcomes.
  • Historical performance should not be treated as a guarantee of future results.
  • Risk tolerance should be considered alongside financial goals and investment horizon.
  • Comparing investments requires more than looking at return percentages alone.

1. The Risk–Return Relationship

Investors generally expect to receive compensation for accepting additional investment risk. This is one reason different asset classes can have very different expected return and risk characteristics.

However, the relationship should not be interpreted as meaning that every risky investment will produce a higher return. Risk represents uncertainty, not a guaranteed reward.

2. Why Return Alone Can Be Misleading

Consider two investments that both generate a 10% return over a particular period. Looking only at the final return makes them appear identical.

But if one investment experienced relatively stable performance while the other experienced significant fluctuations, the investment experience and risk profile were very different.

This is why investment analysis often considers measures of volatility and downside risk in addition to return.

3. Investor Suitability

The same investment may not be equally suitable for every investor. Financial goals, investment horizon, liquidity needs and ability to tolerate losses can influence whether a particular risk level is appropriate.

An investor saving for a near-term requirement may have different priorities from an investor with a long-term wealth-building objective.

4. A Practical Evaluation Framework

Before evaluating an investment, an investor can consider several questions: What is the expected return? What are the major risks? How volatile could the investment be? What is the investment horizon? How would a significant decline affect the investor's financial objective?

These questions encourage a broader assessment instead of focusing on a single performance number.

FinPortfolio Research Note: Risk and return should be evaluated together. A higher expected return may involve greater uncertainty, but higher risk should never be treated as a guarantee of higher performance.
