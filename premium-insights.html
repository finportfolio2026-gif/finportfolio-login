Understanding Risk Before Chasing Returns

A practical look at why return alone is not enough when evaluating an investment decision.

FinPortfolio Premium Insight · Finance & Investing

Why Risk Matters as Much as Return

When people compare investments, the first number they often look at is return. A higher return can certainly look attractive, but return by itself does not tell the complete story.

Two investments can generate similar returns while carrying very different levels of uncertainty. This is why understanding risk is an important part of making an informed investment decision.

Key idea: A good investment decision is not simply about finding the highest return. It is about understanding whether the potential return is reasonable for the level of risk being taken.

1. Return tells you what you gained

Return measures the gain or loss generated by an investment over a particular period. It helps investors understand how an investment has performed.

However, historical or expected return does not guarantee what will happen in the future.

2. Risk tells you about uncertainty

Risk is associated with the uncertainty surrounding an investment's outcome. The actual result can differ from what an investor expects.

Market movements, economic conditions, interest rates, company performance and investor sentiment can all affect investment outcomes.

3. The same return can mean different things

Suppose Investment A and Investment B both generated a 12% return over a particular period.

If Investment A experienced relatively stable movement while Investment B went through significant fluctuations, simply looking at the 12% return would hide an important part of the comparison.

This is why investors should look beyond a single performance number.

4. Risk should match the investor

There is no single level of risk that is suitable for every investor. Investment decisions should consider factors such as financial goals, investment horizon, liquidity requirements and ability to tolerate losses.

A short-term financial goal may require a different approach from a long-term wealth-building objective.

A practical way to think about it

Instead of asking only, "How much can I earn?", an investor can also ask:

What could go wrong? How much fluctuation can I tolerate? How long can I remain invested? Does the potential return justify the uncertainty involved?

FinPortfolio Takeaway: Return helps measure the outcome, but risk helps put that outcome into perspective. Looking at both gives investors a more complete framework for evaluating investment opportunities.
